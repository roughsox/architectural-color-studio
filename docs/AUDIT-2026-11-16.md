# Audit — 16 November 2026

An independent review of the project as it stands at commit `4d682ff`, by the Claude account that wrote the original plan and did not build what followed. Nothing in the product was changed. Scripts behind every measurement are in `research/audit-2026-11-16/`.

## Verdict

The workshop is good. The work it exists to produce has not started.

In two days the project gained a polished public site, 7 palette families, 380 combinations, 1,477 single colours, 40 materials, a scoring system, 8 experts and a notebook of lessons. It has **no finished piece**. The Library is empty, the one project has not moved past its first stage, and the thing the owner wants to post (one image file: a room beside its swatches) has not been defined, composed or exported once.

The project is also carrying three directions at the same time, each replacing the last within a day, none tested to a result: Wada palettes through eight experts; studio-invented palettes with materials and a score; and Trios with a house style. The newest, Trios, has produced no image yet.

The strongest recommendation is narrow: **finish one piece, end to end, before building anything else.** Almost every other finding follows from not having done that.

## What was checked

| Check | How |
|---|---|
| Everything in the repository | Read: STATE, Guide, Notebook, all data files, `build.mjs`, `scheme.js`, the stylesheet, the six planning documents |
| Live site matches the repository | Compared five live pages with a fresh build: identical |
| Every page at phone, iPad and desktop width | 16 pages × 3 widths in a headless browser: script errors, sideways scroll, broken links, tap sizes, text sizes, headings, image descriptions |
| Trio swatch cards | Rendered each card and measured its pixels against the stated colour values |
| The nine room images in the Notebook | Measured how much of each image sits near each palette colour, and its overall colour cast |
| The scheme score | Re-ran it on all 29 studio palettes, on every possible material pair, with small changes to material colours, and on the Wada book |

Not checked: ChatGPT itself (no image was generated for this audit); the owner's seven reference rooms (kept out of the repository); the trend research behind Forecast 2027; the data licences beyond what each package declares; a full accessibility review.

## What is strong

- **The site is well made.** No script errors, no sideways scrolling and no broken links on any page at any width (148 internal links). It is calm, consistent and looks like one hand designed it.
- **Data is plain files with a build that checks them.** Adding a palette is adding a record. A palette that breaks a rule stops the build with a readable reason. This is the right foundation.
- **The Notebook is the most valuable thing in the project.** Twelve lessons, each tied to the attempt that showed it, with the images. It is honest about misses. Lessons 1, 2, 9 and 12 are real findings about how the image maker behaves.
- **The owner's role is clear and small**: three moments, each a short choice.
- **Honesty is built in.** The Guide says plainly that the eight experts are one model in eight roles, and labels material colours as estimates.
- **The palette-family idea works.** It gives the owner something to browse and play with, which was the brief for the workshop half of the project.

## Findings

Ranked by how much they limit the project.

### 1. No finished piece exists, and "finished" is not defined

- Library: 0 pieces. Project 001: stage 1 of 7 since yesterday; the Architect's turn never happened; no sit-down has been held.
- The shareable image has no specification. The prompts ask for a 4:5 room. A 4:5 room with a swatch card beside it is roughly square or wider, which takes less of a phone screen than the 4:5 the platforms favour. Whether the post is one composed image, a two-slide carousel, or a room with a swatch strip has not been decided.
- There are two different swatch designs: a flat strip beside the room (Library page) and a textured card with engraved names (Trios). They cannot both be "the piece".
- No code composes or exports the final image. The Library page lays out a room and swatches in the browser; nothing produces the file to post.

### 2. Two workflows that do not touch

| | The designed workflow | The real workflow |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Current project page: stages, eight experts, sit-down, "your turn" | Notebook: screenshot a card, paste a prompt in ChatGPT, regenerate, pick |
| Used | Three notes, then stopped | Nine images across two palettes |
| Measures the image | Designed to, never has | No |
| Records an approval | Designed to | No |
| Leads to the Library | Yes, in principle | No path |

The palettes actually being tested (Plum Drench, Emerald Sofa) have no project. The project that exists (Wada 231) has no images. The two "good" images have nowhere to go. The experts, the stages and the sit-down are, today, ceremony: none has influenced an image.

### 3. Three directions, each contradicting the last

The Guide (v0.8) describes all three at once and contradicts itself:

| The Guide says | The Trios format and the owner's taste notes say |
|---|---|
| "Neutral daylight, so the palette colours are not shifted by warm light" (§9, and the Photographer's job) | "Warm, golden lamplight" is wanted |
| "Not symmetrical" (§9) | "One centred focal wall, shot straight on" |
| "Calm architecture; the colour is the spectacle" (§9) | Panelling, arched lit built-ins, brass, chandeliers |
| The Field colour is "the big, quiet colour"; in 25 of 29 studio palettes it is the lightest colour | "The hero colour is usually deep and saturated, and it dominates" |
| Two natural materials, scored, are part of every scheme (§7) | Trios have no materials and no score |

The 29 studio palettes were built on an assumption (the lightest colour covers the room) that the owner's references then contradicted. Whether those palettes are kept, recast or retired is listed as an open question in the Notebook and nowhere decided.

### 4. Verdicts are not tied to the palette

Measured on the nine Notebook images (share of the picture within a fixed distance of each palette colour; a rough whole-image measure, because light and shadow move pixel values):

| Image | Verdict | Field colour | Accent | Outside the palette |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plum Drench 1 | miss | 7% | 3% | 33% |
| Plum Drench 3 | miss | **35%** | 1% | 39% |
| Plum Drench 5 | miss | 3% | 2% | 70% |
| Plum Drench 7 | **good** | 8% | 1% | 38% |
| Plum Drench 8 | **good** | 11% | 1% | 45% |
| Emerald Sofa 1 | miss | 17% | 0% | 43% |

- The two "good" images carry no more of the palette than the misses. Attempt 7 has almost the same colour distribution as attempt 1, the first miss. The verdicts are judging whether it is a handsome room, which is legitimate, but it is not the stated purpose: "the room shows what a colour combination feels like."
- **Every image has the same warm cast**, including those prompted for neutral or true colour. The brightest 5% of each picture, where white should be, is clearly yellow in all nine.
- **All nine look alike.** Amber cove and shelf lighting, a lit fire, candles, a landscape painting, a trailing plant, a round dark coffee table, a chunky mustard throw. These recur across different prompts, chats and palettes. This is the image maker's default interior, and it is recognisable as generated. The Notebook's "still to work out" list names it; it is the main quality problem, not a footnote.
- The Notebook says "about one in two or three attempts is good." The record shows 2 good, 2 closer and 5 misses out of 9.
- Images are stored at 800 × 1000. Guide §2 says "the original image is stored, untouched", and 800 px is below what a feed post needs. The originals are not in the project.

### 5. The swatch card does not show the colours it states

Guide §10: "the swatch panel is drawn from the palette's exact values… the one part of the piece that is exactly right." Measured on the rendered Trio cards (ΔE00: about 1 is invisible, 2–3 is noticeable side by side, above 5 is plainly a different shade):

| Swatch | Finish | Stated | Rendered | ΔE00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sage | plaster | `#8E9A7E` | `#A7B09A` | **7.3** |
| Cognac | leather | `#9A5A2E` | `#A96B3B` | **6.4** |
| Ink | plaster | `#1F2E3F` | `#283A4F` | 4.1 |
| Aubergine | lacquer | `#4A2A3E` | `#533346` | 2.8 |
| Bone, Chalk, Ivory | linen, boucle | — | — | 1.8–2.0 |
| Saffron, Rose | velvet | — | — | 0.5–0.6 |

The plaster and leather textures lighten the colour. The card is what the image maker is shown, so it is being asked to match a shade the palette does not contain.

Also on the card:
- **Names are hard to read on mid and light swatches.** Contrast of the engraved name against its swatch: Cognac 2.2:1, Bone 2.8:1, Chalk 3.0:1 (4.5:1 is the usual minimum for text). The finish label is 9.5 px at 75% opacity.
- **The typeface depends on the device.** The card asks for Didot or Bodoni and falls back to Georgia where those are missing, so the same card looks different on an iPhone, an Android phone and a Windows laptop.
- **It is delivered by screenshot**, so its size, crop and colour handling vary with every capture.

### 6. The score is less solid than it looks

The scheme score gates publication at 85 and is described as "established colour-design practice, made measurable."

- **It is unstable.** Material colours are estimates ("to be replaced by measurements"). Moving each material only halfway toward the light or dark end of its own stated range drops **15 of the 29** published palettes below 85. Chocolate Drench goes from 93 to 40; Smoky Blue from 98 to 57. A real piece of walnut varies more than that.
- **The palette rules would refuse half of the Wada book.** 178 of the 348 Wada combinations fail the studio's own rules (120 on the light-dark gap, 80 on "one loud colour"). The rules describe one style, not colour practice in general.
- **Nothing has tested it against the owner's eye.** All 29 palettes score 86–98 because they were adjusted until they passed. No palette the owner disliked has been scored, so there is no evidence the number tracks taste.
- **It does not apply to the current format.** Trios are checked only for two colour distances.
- 23 studio colour names still name an object or finish (Mustard Throw, Emerald Velvet, Oxblood Chair, Honey Plaster, Garnet Lacquer…). Lesson 12 found that "Walnut Shadow" turned into a walnut wall; the build check added afterwards covers woods, stones and metals but not these.

### 7. The record has drifted from the project

- **`STATE.md` is 26 KB and its opening is wrong.** "Planning, plus a first readable slice… the four-page site is not built"; "Project 001 is a placeholder". The true state is only in the log entries at the bottom. Its eleven open decisions are from the first plan (providers, budgets, the "Plate") and no longer apply.
- **`README.md`** says "Status: planning. No product code yet."
- **`AGENTS.md` and `STATE.md` disagree** on publishing. AGENTS: publishing "needs the owner's go-ahead for that change." STATE: "publish every checked change… don't ask first."
- **Six planning documents** (about 760 lines) are listed as current reading. Most of what they describe was superseded: paid image providers, blind reviewers, cross-model confirmation, the 9:16 Plate.
- `SOURCES.md` does not list the Trios or Materials families.
- "Private" reference notes on 18 studio colours are published in the public data file. They point to open sources today, so there is no harm yet, but the owner has said paint-brand references should stay private.

### 8. Site details

Passing: no errors, no sideways scroll, no broken links, one main heading per page, images described.

### 9. Engineering and operations

- **Publishing is manual.** The Vercel project still tracks `main`, so every change needs a hand-made production deployment. At least fifteen of those happened in one day; each is a chance to forget.
- **No tests.** The build's checks are the only safety net, and the scoring code has none of its own. The colour maths exists twice (`build.mjs` and `scheme.js`).
- **Images live in git.** Nine images are 1.4 MB. Thousands of pieces with several attempts each would be gigabytes; git and the build's copy step are the wrong home for that.
- `build.mjs` is one 440-line file of nested templates. It works and it is readable enough today; it will be the first thing to slow down changes.
- The Trio prompt exists in two places (the build and the Notebook). They match today.

### 10. Risks to the goal

- **Distinctiveness.** The Trios format and house style were taken from other people's posts: the same three-colour textured card beside the same style of room. Made well, it will look like those accounts. The project has not yet decided what is its own.
- **Sameness at scale.** One house style, one camera angle and one image maker with a strong default look, repeated a thousand times.
- **The loop is manual per image.** Screenshot, new chat, paste, regenerate, save, bring back. At the measured hit rate that is roughly four to five generations per keeper.
- **The workshop is absorbing the effort.** 23 commits since the skeleton; none produced a piece.

## How to make it better

In order. The first three matter more than the rest together.

### 1. Finish one piece

Take one Trio. Generate until one room is good. Put it beside its card. Export one image file. Put it in the Library. Look at it on a phone.

This forces the decisions that are open: the shape of the post, which swatch design, how the image arrives, what "approved" means. Until one exists, every other improvement is a guess about a product nobody has seen.

**Decision for the owner:** one composed image, or a two-slide carousel (room, then card)? Recommendation: the carousel as the default, with the room at full 4:5, and a composed single image as a second export. It gives the room the whole screen and removes the layout problem.

### 2. One workflow

Merge the project page and the Notebook loop into one record per piece:

1. **Palette** chosen (the owner's first moment).
2. **Card and prompt** issued by the studio.
3. **Attempts**: every image that comes back is logged against the piece, measured on arrival, with a verdict.
4. **Review**: the experts speak here, on a real image, where they have something to judge. Three or four short notes, not eight.
5. **Approval** (the owner's last moment), then the Library.

Keep the experts; the owner enjoys them. Move them from before the image, where the Notebook found that planning hurts ("the more structure a prompt has, the more the image becomes a demonstration of the structure"), to after it, where judgment is useful.

### 3. Choose one format and test it properly

Commit to Trios for now. Park the four-colour studio palettes and the materials score; do not delete them.

Run the test the Notebook already proposes, with a fixed protocol:

- 3 trios × 3 generations, same prompt, new chat each time, **every** result logged including failures.
- Before looking, write down what counts as a hit: five yes-or-no questions (hero colour on the walls and reads as its swatch; upholstery colour present and reads as its swatch; whites look white; no item from the "defaults" list; you would post it).
- The answer is a hit rate with a number, per trio and overall. That number decides whether the prompt, the card or the method changes next.

### 4. Fix the card

- Texture as a lightness-only layer that averages to nothing, so each swatch measures as its stated colour. Add a build check that renders the card and fails if any swatch is more than 2 ΔE00 out.
- Names in a text colour chosen per swatch for at least 4.5:1 contrast. Larger finish labels.
- Ship the typeface with the site so the card is identical everywhere.
- A **Download card** button that saves a fixed-size image. No screenshots.

### 5. Measure every image when it arrives

The script written for this audit does it in a second. Shown beside each attempt: how much of the picture is each palette colour, how much is outside the palette, and whether the whites are white. It will not replace the owner's eye; it will stop "good" drifting away from "shows the palette".

Keep originals at full size from now on.

### 6. Name the defaults and fight them

Start a list in the Notebook of what the image maker reaches for unasked: amber cove lighting, lit fire, candles on a tray, hydrangeas, trailing pothos, a landscape painting, a round dark coffee table, a chunky knit throw. Treat "none of these" as part of a hit. Then test, one at a time, what removes them without re-creating the rule-list problem from lesson 1: a reference photograph attached with the card is the first thing to try.

### 7. Demote the score until it is validated

- Show it as advice, not a gate. Remove "established colour-design practice".
- Validate it: have the owner sort 20 schemes into like and dislike without seeing scores, then compare.
- Replace estimated material colours with measured ones before trusting any threshold.

### 8. Make the house style the studio's own

The references are a starting point. Decide what is deliberately different: the card design, the naming voice, one signature element in every room, the caption. Write it in the Guide as the studio's house style rather than a description of other accounts.

### 9. Clean the record

- Rewrite `STATE.md` to one screen: what the project is now, the current method, what is open, how to publish. Move the log to its own file.
- Guide v1.0 around the chosen format, with the contradictions in finding 3 resolved.
- Move the six planning documents to `docs/archive/` with a note on what survived.
- Fix `README.md`, `SOURCES.md` and the publishing rule in `AGENTS.md`.

### 10. Remove the manual publishing step

Set the Vercel project's production branch to `architectural-color-studio`. A push then publishes. This is one setting in the Vercel dashboard.

### 11. Site polish

Larger navigation tap targets and a phone menu that stays on one row; nothing under 12 px; darken the grey text slightly; break the Wada page into pages or load it in parts; add a site icon; decide whether search engines should index the workshop, and keep reference notes out of the public data.

### 12. Later, when volume matters

- Image storage outside git before the Library passes about a hundred pieces.
- A few tests for the colour maths and the scoring, and one shared colour module.
- If the manual loop becomes the limit, the paid image interface removes it entirely. That is a decision about volume, not quality; nothing above depends on it.

## What to stop, for now

- New palette families and new series. There are 380 combinations and no rooms.
- New pages.
- Tuning the score.
- Holding sit-downs before an image exists.

## Addendum: what this audit missed

Found by the owner the same day. The trio page labelled the hero colour "Walls and built-in joinery", and the trio prompt said "the top colour on the walls and the built-in joinery". The image maker reads a colour assigned to joinery as an instruction to build joinery, which is why a bedroom got two walls of library shelving. This audit read the page and the prompt and did not catch it. It is fixed (Notebook lesson 18).

The audit also understated finding 9. Publishing was not merely manual: every change produced two deployments (an automatic preview on push, then a hand-made production one), across two accounts, until Vercel's free limit of 100 deployments a day was hit and publishing stopped. The fix is to make `architectural-color-studio` the project's production branch, so one push is one deployment.

## One-line summary

The workshop is built; now make one piece, fix the card, measure what comes back, and let the hit rate decide what to build next.
